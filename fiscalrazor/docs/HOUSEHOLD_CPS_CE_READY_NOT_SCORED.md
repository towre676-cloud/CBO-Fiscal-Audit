# Household data and Consumer Expenditure correction — ready for original files

## Official public microdata present on the internet, not in this archive

**Census CPS ASEC 2026**: https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2026/demo/cps/cps-asec-2026.html . Original public-use CSV ZIP is `https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cps/datasets/2026/march/asecpub26csv.zip` (**includes replicate weights**), around 132 MB; the separate 2026 ASCII replicate-weight ZIP is `https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cps/datasets/2026/march/CPS_ASEC_ASCII_REPWGT_2026.ZIP`. The published technical documentation is `https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cps/techdocs/cpsmar26.pdf`. Its abstract provides record-count targets: 88,026 household, 61,133 family and 134,729 person SAS/CSV records, not a set of observations fabricated in FiscalRazor.

On a network-connected computer use `python scripts/fetch_sources.py --id cps_asec_2026_csv --allow-large`; it saves the original ZIP, does not overwrite another file on checksum failure, and writes an acquisition receipt. Next run `python scripts/preflight_cps_2026.py --list`, inspect actual member names and supply the three CSV members explicitly. The tool streams the real rows and requires their counts to match the technical documentation. **None of these original records is included in v0.4**.

**BLS Consumer Expenditure Public Use Microdata**: https://www.bls.gov/cex/pumd_data.htm . The 2025 lapse in appropriations means October/November 2025 data were not collected; BLS's published 2025 adjustments were NOT applied to PUMD. The specific procedure: https://www.bls.gov/cex/ce-pumd-2025-shutdown-impact.htm . `src/fiscal_data/ce_2025_adjustments.py` implements the published Interview adjustments: multiply the July and October **2025 expense months** by 3/2, August and September by 3; adjust MO_SCOPE in 2025 interview months 8, 9, 12 to 3.5, 5.5, 5.5 and in January 2026 to 3.5. Diary survey September and December 2025 interview/diary-start weights and all 44 replicates are doubled. The module rejects a second application. The modified PUMD estimates will still differ from published national statistics because of suppression/topcoding, imputation, and missing data.

A household-level net reform effect requires the tax unit, household membership, consumption, taxes, transfers, eligibility, demographics, geographic data, survey weights, and tax-benefit models all from validated sources. The existing `fiscal_data.distribution` analyzer only accepts **actual modeled before-and-after record results**; it does not assume CPS income alone determines tax burden. The 160-replicate sampling variance protocol remains a guard; without all 160 genuine estimates and their joins, **no national incidence statistics are reported**. This is a required future output, not an accomplishment claimed here.
